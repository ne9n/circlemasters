## 1. Introduction: Tangible Flight on the Pioneer Turf
Every summer, more than 600,000 aviation enthusiasts travel from around the world to Wittman Regional Airport in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, for **EAA AirVenture**. While the skies above the main flight line roar with modern fighters, vintage warbirds, and experimental homebuilts, one of the most transformative hands-on aviation experiences takes place at **KidVenture**, situated across the grass of historic Pioneer Airport (and expanding into Discovery Park).

As noted by renowned aerodynamicist and aeronautical engineer **Barnaby Wainfan** in *KITPLANES Magazine*, KidVenture gives youth an authentic, physical introduction to the laws of aerodynamics. While children can build air rockets, buck rivets, and try computer simulators across the KidVenture grounds, it is on the **manicured grass control line circles** where flight theory turns into direct physical sensation.

Gripping a control handle tethered to dual stainless-steel flying lines, a young pilot experiences:
* **The Centrifugal Pull:** The outward physical tension generated by circular flight speed.
* **Direct Elevator Authority:** The immediate mechanical pitch response transferred through wrist movements to the bellcrank and elevator.
* **The Thrill of Command:** Complete, real-time control over an airborne machine.

## 2. A 30-Year Heritage: Over 25,000 First Flights
What began decades ago as an informal demonstration by Wisconsin modelers has grown into an internationally recognized flight-training operation sanctioned and celebrated by both the **Experimental Aircraft Association (EAA)** and the **Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA)**:

* **Over Three Decades of Dedication:** Coordinated in deep partnership with the **Circle Masters Flying Club** and championed by longtime organizers, pilots, and innovators including **Doug & Geri MacArthur**, **Chris Sterner**, **Dave Siegler**, **DC Jensen**, **Steve Mills**, **Dan McEntee**, and **Sean McEntee**, along with volunteer aeromodelers from across North America.
* **Over 25,000 First Flights:** Spanning generations of AirVenture attendees, the volunteer team has provided more than 25,000 instructional flights to young pilots and curious adults.
* **Seven Days of Non-Stop Operation:** Operating two active circles side-by-side from 9:00 AM to 3:30 PM Monday through Saturday, and concluding with a high-tempo Sunday session until 2:00 PM.

## 3. The Volunteer Engine: Camp Scholler, Camaraderie & BBQs
Running continuous flight operations under the summer heat requires a dedicated, well-drilled volunteer corps. Enthusiasts who connect year-round on forums like **StuntHanger.com** converge at Oshkosh to donate their time and energy:

* **Basecamp at Camp Scholler:** Volunteers establish a shared camp compound in Camp Scholler, complete with community breakfasts, evening debriefs, and legendary "hangar flying" sessions under the stars.
* **The Annual Wednesday Volunteer BBQ:** A cherished AirVenture tradition bringing together pit crews, battery managers, and flight coaches to celebrate their shared mission.
* **The "Hands-on-Handle" Instructional Method:** Every student is paired one-on-one with a veteran pilot who stands directly in the center of the circle, resting a steadying hand over the student’s on the handle. The instructor guides gentle wrist movement, prevents over-controlling, and ensures that the student remains in safe, confident command.

## 4. Volunteer Profiles: Voices from the Flight Line

### Geri MacArthur — National Volunteer Coordinator
* **Role:** Operations & Volunteer Leadership, KidVenture Control Line
* **Impact:** Directs the national volunteer corps, coordinating schedules for dozens of instructors arriving from across North America. Geri manages the 4-hour daily volunteer rotations, safety briefings, and the legendary Wednesday night Volunteer BBQ. Under her and Doug MacArthur's leadership, the KidVenture CL area has become a welcoming family for volunteers and attendees alike.

### Dan and Sean McEntee — Multi-Generational Flight Line Champions
* **Role:** Flight Coaches, Technical Pit Crew & StuntHanger Community Leaders
* **Impact:** Longtime mainstays of the control line community, Dan and Sean bring immense practical knowledge, motor tuning, and hands-on coaching to the circles. Working side-by-side on the turf at Pioneer Airport, they guide beginners through their first flights, troubleshoot airframes, and foster the family-oriented spirit that defines the hobby.

### The Late Arthur Johnson (AMA #1825) — Passing the Aeromodeling Torch
* **Role:** In Memoriam — Veteran Flight Instructor & AMA District VI Control Line Contest Director (AMA Member since 1953)
* **Legacy & Perspective:** With more than 60 years of aeromodeling expertise, the late Arthur Johnson was a passionate champion for youth outreach. For Arthur, the true reward of Oshkosh was witnessing the "lightbulb moment" when a child stepped into the circle, overcame their hesitation, and experienced pure flight command on the wires. His warmth and encouragement inspired countess young modelers.

### The Late Robert "Bob" Arata (AMA #8786) — Decades of Dedicated Service
* **Role:** In Memoriam — Flight Instructor, USAF Veteran & Master Aircraft Mechanic
* **Legacy & Perspective:** A fixture on the Pioneer Airport turf for decades, the late Bob Arata utilized his extensive aircraft mechanic background and steady coaching demeanor to guide thousands of first-time flyers. Bob’s emphasis on the dual-handle technique ensured that every child enjoyed a safe, confidence-building flight, establishing a standard of instruction that lives on today.

## 5. Engineering the Ultimate Trainer: Chris Sterner's "Eagle One"
Operating high-tempo rotations with hundreds of first-time pilots each day means aircraft must withstand what Barnaby Wainfan aptly described as "unplanned intersections with the ground." 

To solve this challenge, designer **Chris Sterner** engineered the **Eagle One Trainer**:

```
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                         EAGLE ONE TRAINER SPECIFICATIONS                   │
 ├────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Wingspan                   │ 30-1/4 inches                                 │
 │ Wing Area                  │ 225 sq. inches                                │
 │ Airframe Material          │ 0.158″ Fluted Polypropylene (Coroplast)       │
 │ Assembly Method            │ High-Strength Hot-Melt Adhesive               │
 │ Power System               │ 2807 1300KV Brushless Outrunner & ESC         │
 │ Flight Timing Unit         │ Custom Solid-State Electronic Timer (D. Siegler)│
 │ Controls                   │ 1/2-A Bellcrank, 1/16″ Music Wire Pushrod     │
 │ Quick-Swap Capability      │ Rapid-disconnect power & control mountings    │
 └────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Evolution: From Cox .049s to Brushless Electric
* **The Classic Glow Era:** In earlier years, trainers relied on the venerable **Cox .049 Reed-Valve engine**, introducing kids to the nostalgic sound and scent of glow fuel.
* **The Electric Transition:** To maximize turnaround speed and eliminate messy oil cleanup and hot engine restarts, the fleet modernized with **brushless electric motors** and **custom electronic timers** (designed with Dave Siegler). The timers deliver a soft-start ramp, consistent RPM, and a predictable soft cutoff for landing.
* **Field Repairability:** Built entirely of tough Coroplast (the fluted plastic used in outdoor yard signs), the airframes flex rather than shatter on rough landings. Spare airframes and quick-mount systems allow ground crews to have planes back in the air in minutes.

## 7. The Flight Experience: From Takeoff to Stunt Airshow

```
 +────────────────────────+     +────────────────────────+     +────────────────────────+
 │  1. PRE-FLIGHT BRIEF   │     │  2. FLIGHT IN COMMAND  │     │ 3. PRECISION AIRSHOWS  │
 │ • Wrist up / Wrist down│     │ • Electric soft-start  │     │ • Stunt demonstrations │
 │ • Maintaining tension  │ ==> │ • Liftoff in 1/2 lap   │ ==> │ • Inside/outside loops │
 │ • Body rotation basics │     │ • Level flight mastery │     │ • Inverted & 8-figures │
 +────────────────────────+     +────────────────────────+     +────────────────────────+
```

1. **Pre-Flight Briefing:** Ground crew instructors explain the basic mechanics: tilting the wrist backward pulls the top line, raising the elevator to climb; tilting forward commands a descent.
2. **The First Flight:** As the electric timer engages and the propeller spins up, the pit crew releases the model. Within half a lap, the Eagle One lifts off. The immediate tactile feedback—feeling the tension and hearing the motor hum on the wire—creates an instant, memorable connection to flight.
3. **Precision Aerobatics (CL Stunt) Airshows:** Between training flights, master aeromodelers take to the circle flying full-size competition stunt aircraft on 60-foot lines. Performing the official AMA Precision Aerobatics pattern—consecutive inside loops, inverted horizontal flight, vertical eights, and the cloverleaf—these demonstrations show young pilots where their new skills can take them.

## 8. Sustaining the Spark: Taking Flight Home
KidVenture’s mission is to launch lifelong aviation journeys:

* **Free Eagle One Plans:** Complete construction drawings, templates, and bills of materials for the Eagle One are distributed **free of charge** at [Circle Masters Flying Club](https://circlemasters.com) and [KV Control Line](https://kvcontrolline.weebly.com/) so families can build their own trainers at home.
* **Local Mentorship:** Families are directed to local chartered **Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA)** clubs and online modeling communities such as [StuntHanger.com](https://stunthanger.com) to find local mentors, building assistance, and flying sites.
* **The "Great Works of Flight" Gallery:** Builders who complete an Eagle One at home are invited to share their accomplishments with the KidVenture team by emailing photos to `kidventurecl@outlook.com`.

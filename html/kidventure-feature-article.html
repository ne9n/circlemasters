Flying on the Wires of Oshkosh: The Story and Spirit of Control Line Aviation at EAA KidVenture

How dedicated aeromodeling volunteers, the Eagle One trainer, and hands-on flight coaching have introduced over 25,000 youth to flight at EAA AirVenture.
Published by Circle Masters Flying Club & Aeromodeling Community Contributors · References: EAA KidVenture, KV Control Line, KITPLANES Magazine, AMA Blog & StuntHanger.com
"Planting the seed of interest in aviation, one kid at a time!"
— EAA KidVenture Control Line Team Motto

1. Tangible Flight on the Pioneer Turf

Every summer, more than 600,000 aviation enthusiasts travel from across the globe to Wittman Regional Airport in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, for EAA AirVenture. While the main flight line roars with modern fighters, vintage warbirds, and experimental homebuilts, one of the most transformative hands-on aviation experiences takes place at KidVenture, situated across the grass of historic Pioneer Airport (and expanding into Discovery Park).

As noted by renowned aerodynamicist and aeronautical engineer Barnaby Wainfan in KITPLANES Magazine, KidVenture gives youth an authentic, physical introduction to the laws of aerodynamics. While children can build air rockets, buck rivets, and try computer simulators across the KidVenture grounds, it is on the manicured grass control line circles where flight theory turns into direct physical sensation.

Gripping a control handle tethered to dual stainless-steel flying lines, a young pilot experiences:

  • The Centrifugal Pull: The outward physical tension generated by circular flight speed.
  • Direct Elevator Authority: The immediate mechanical pitch response transferred through wrist movements to the bellcrank and elevator.
  • The Thrill of Command: Complete, real-time control over an airborne machine.

2. A 30-Year Heritage: Over 25,000 First Flights

What began decades ago as an informal demonstration by Wisconsin modelers has grown into an internationally recognized flight-training operation sanctioned and celebrated by both the Experimental Aircraft Association (EAA) and the Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA):

  • Over Three Decades of Dedication: Coordinated in deep partnership with the Circle Masters Flying Club and championed by longtime organizers, pilots, and innovators including Doug & Geri MacArthur, Chris Sterner, Dave Siegler, DC Jensen, Steve Mills, Dan McEntee, and Sean McEntee, along with volunteer aeromodelers from across North America.
  • Over 25,000 First Flights: Spanning generations of AirVenture attendees, the volunteer team has provided more than 25,000 instructional flights to young pilots and curious adults.
  • A Global Destination for Youth: With AirVenture drawing attendees from more than 80 countries, the Pioneer Airport circles become a melting pot of international aviation enthusiasm. Children and families visiting from Europe, South America, Asia, Australia, and Africa step up to fly side-by-side, united by the universal language of model aviation and the thrill of holding the lines.
  • Seven Days of Non-Stop Operation: Operating two active circles side-by-side from 9:00 AM to 3:30 PM Monday through Saturday, and concluding with a high-tempo Sunday session until 2:00 PM.

3. The Volunteer Engine: Camp Scholler & The Community

Running continuous flight operations under the summer heat requires a dedicated, well-drilled volunteer corps. Enthusiasts who connect year-round on forums like StuntHanger.com converge at Oshkosh to donate their time and energy:

  • Basecamp at Camp Scholler: Volunteers establish a shared camp compound in Camp Scholler, complete with community breakfasts, evening debriefs, and legendary "hangar flying" sessions under the stars.
  • The Annual Wednesday Volunteer BBQ: A cherished AirVenture tradition bringing together pit crews, battery managers, and flight coaches to celebrate their shared mission.
  • The "Hands-on-Handle" Instructional Method: Every student is paired one-on-one with a veteran pilot who stands directly in the center of the circle, resting a steadying hand over the student’s on the handle. The instructor guides gentle wrist movement, prevents over-controlling, and ensures that the student remains in safe, confident command.

4. Volunteer Profiles: Voices from the Flight Line

Geri MacArthur

National Volunteer Coordinator

Directs the national volunteer corps, coordinating schedules for dozens of instructors arriving from across North America. Geri manages the 4-hour daily volunteer rotations, safety briefings, and the legendary Wednesday night Volunteer BBQ. Under her and Doug MacArthur's leadership, the KidVenture CL area has become a welcoming family for volunteers and attendees alike.

Dan and Sean McEntee

Flight Coaches & Technical Pit Crew

Well-known leaders in the control line community and active StuntHanger contributors, Dan and Sean McEntee represent the family-driven heart of aeromodeling. Bringing deep technical craftsmanship, engine/motor tuning, and patient coaching to Pioneer Airport, the McEntees work the circles all week long to ensure every child experiences the thrill of flight safely and successfully.

The Late Arthur Johnson (AMA #1825)

In Memoriam · Veteran Instructor & AMA District VI Contest Director

An AMA member since 1953 with more than 60 years of aeromodeling expertise, the late Arthur Johnson was a beloved pillar of youth outreach. Arthur cherished the "lightbulb moment" when a child stepped into the circle, overcame their hesitation, and experienced pure flight command on the wires. His warmth, generosity, and lifelong advocacy continue to inspire the team.

The Late Robert "Bob" Arata (AMA #8786)

In Memoriam · Flight Instructor, USAF Veteran & Master Mechanic

A fixture on the Pioneer Airport turf for decades, the late Bob Arata utilized his extensive aircraft mechanic background and calm, encouraging coaching demeanor to guide thousands of first-time flyers. Bob’s mastery of the dual-handle technique ensured that every child enjoyed a safe, confidence-building flight, creating memories that lasted a lifetime.

5. Engineering the Ultimate Trainer: Chris Sterner's "Eagle One"

Operating high-tempo rotations with hundreds of first-time pilots each day means aircraft must withstand what Barnaby Wainfan aptly described as "unplanned intersections with the ground." Designer Chris Sterner engineered the Eagle One Trainer specifically to meet this demanding challenge:

Specification Details
Wingspan 30-1/4 inches
Wing Area 225 sq. inches
Airframe Material 0.158″ Fluted Polypropylene (Coroplast)
Assembly Method High-Strength Hot-Melt Adhesive along all seams
Power System 2807 1300KV Brushless Outrunner Motor & ESC
Flight Timing Unit Custom Solid-State Electronic Timer (developed with Dave Siegler)
Control Hardware 1/2-A Bellcrank, 1/16″ Music Wire Pushrod, 1/2-A Handle

The Transition to Electric Power: While earlier decades relied on legendary Cox .049 glow engines, the fleet modernized to brushless electric power. This transition eliminated hot-engine restarts, messy fuel residue, and needle valve adjustments, while electronic timers ensure consistent RPM and a predictable soft cutoff for landing.

7. The Flight Experience & World-Class Aerobatics

1. Pre-Flight Orientation: Ground crew instructors explain the physics: tilting the wrist backward pulls the top line, raising the elevator to climb; tilting forward commands a descent.

2. The First Flight: As the electric timer engages and the propeller spins up, the pit crew releases the model. Within half a lap, the Eagle One lifts off. The immediate tactile feedback—feeling the tension and hearing the motor hum on the wire—creates an instant, memorable connection to flight.

3. Precision Aerobatics (CL Stunt) Airshows & Champion Mentors: Between training flights, master modelers, national champions, and international competitors take to the circle flying full-size competition stunt aircraft on 60-foot lines:

  • Gilbert & Véronique Béringer: The founders of Beringer Aero and acclaimed international aerobatic competitors from France, showcasing European precision aerobatics and global passion for aeromodeling.
  • Tom Siegler: Multi-time national competitor and champion Control Line Combat pilot, showcasing the extreme speed, razor-sharp agility, and high-energy excitement of CL Combat.
  • Burt Rutan, Sean Elliott & Aviation Leadership: Legendary aerospace designer Burt Rutan, EAA Vice President of Advocacy and Safety Sean Elliott, and other aviation luminaries have visited and supported the circle, celebrating control line flying as a cornerstone of aerodynamic intuition and hands-on piloting instinct.
  • The Complete AMA Pattern: Demonstrators carve through inside/outside loops, inverted horizontal passes, vertical eights, and cloverleafs, showing youth the breathtaking heights achievable in the hobby.

8. Sustaining the Spark: Taking Flight Home

KidVenture’s mission is to launch lifelong aviation journeys:

  • Free Eagle One Plans: Complete construction drawings, templates, and bills of materials for the Eagle One are distributed free of charge at Circle Masters Flying Club and KV Control Line so families can build their own trainers at home.
  • Local Mentorship: Families are directed to local chartered Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA) clubs and online modeling communities such as StuntHanger.com to find local mentors and flying sites.
  • The "Great Works of Flight" Gallery: Builders who complete an Eagle One at home are invited to share photos of their models by emailing the team at kidventurecl@outlook.com.

1. Overview & Flight Mechanics

At EAA AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, the Control Line flight area at Pioneer Airport provides hands-on flight training. Unlike computer simulators, control line flying gives immediate physical feedback:

  • Aerodynamic Control: Dual steel lines connect a control handle to an internal bellcrank and elevator pushrod.
  • Pitch Control: Tilting the wrist backward commands elevator up (climb); tilting forward commands elevator down (dive).
  • Line Tension: Centrifugal force maintains line tension, giving the student pilot physical sensation of aerodynamic forces and motor thrust throughout the flight.

2. Origins, Operations & Airspace Fit

  • Origins & Early History: Control line flying at EAA AirVenture predates KidVenture. Seeking hands-on youth activities, Sean Elliott (former Junior Nats Stunt Champion and future EAA VP) partnered with Jim Krueger and the Milwaukee Circle Masters. At the annual SIG contest in Montezuma, Iowa, Krueger recruited Dan McEntee and fellow St. Louis modelers (including Bob Arata and Jan Potts) to establish the volunteer corps.
  • Flight Volume: Over 25,000 instructional flights logged across 30+ years, averaging 1,400 to 1,800 youth flights each week and peaking at 2,500 flights in a single year.
  • Schedule: Monday through Saturday: 9:00 AM – 3:30 PM; Sunday: 9:00 AM – 2:00 PM, split into 4-hour morning and afternoon shifts.
  • Two Turf Circles: Simultaneous continuous operations staffed by dedicated flight coaches, pit mechanics, and registration coordinators.
  • Hand-Launch Operations: Aircraft omit landing gear for grass durability; ground crew members hand-launch each plane into the wind.
  • Field & Weather Conditions: Operated over hard-packed turf facing frequently hot, humid, and windy summer weather conditions across Pioneer Airport.
  • Tight Airspace Compatibility: Control line is uniquely suited to EAA's congested airspace. Because airplanes remain physically tethered to a fixed 50–60 ft radius, flights stay contained within a safe, predictable volume without conflicting with nearby full-scale aircraft traffic.
  • Adjacent to Bell 47 Helicopter Runway: The circles operate immediately adjacent to the active Pioneer Airport turf runway and helipad where iconic Bell 47 helicopters continuously take off and land giving passenger rides throughout the day.
  • International Reach: Serves youth and families from across North America and over 80 countries worldwide.

3. Trainer Airframe Evolution & The Eagle 1

The training fleet evolved across five distinct generations:

  1. Original Club Models (Early 1990s): Initial demonstration flights using volunteers' personal balsa aircraft.
  2. Cox Plastic Ready-to-Fly (RTF): Commercial plastic trainers (such as the Cox PT-19) powered by Cox .049 glow engines.
  3. Bratco Skybabies (SIG Manufacturing): Adopted Bratco Skybaby airplanes, supplied and supported by SIG Manufacturing. While Norvel .061 engines were exceptionally reliable, high-tempo student flying caused fragile balsa/foam airframes to break at a rate of 2 to 3 per day.
  4. The "Tuf Baby" (Bob Arata & Dan McEntee, 2010): Conceived by Bob Arata and Dan McEntee on the drive home to St. Louis to solve the breakage problem (plans drawn by Art Johnson). Bob Arata built the original five Coroplast Tuf Babies in 2010; they flew thousands of flights over multiple seasons without retiring a single plane, greatly easing maintenance for Art Johnson.
  5. The "Eagle 1" (Chris Sterner): Modern purpose-built electric Coroplast trainer designed by Chris Sterner, optimized for brushless power and Dave Siegler electronic timers.

Engine & Sponsor Progression:

  • Cox .049 Era: Foundation years powered by Cox reed-valve engines with factory engine and plug support.
  • Norvel .061 Era: Transitioned to high-output **Norvel .061 Big Mig** engines (6x3 props) and **Skybaby** airplanes, supplied and supported by **SIG Manufacturing** alongside fuel, building supplies, and hardware.
  • Electric Era: Modern fleet powered by brushless motors and **Thunder Power RC** LiPo batteries, controlled by custom Siegler electronic timers for zero-mess, high-tempo operations.

Multi-Generational Legacy & Aviation Alumni

Alumni Impact

Parents who flew at KidVenture as kids decades ago now return with their own children. Many alumni have gone on to careers as airline captains, military aviators, and aeronautical engineers, returning to Pioneer Airport to credit their control line experience with sparking their aviation careers.

Always Welcoming New Volunteers — Join the Flight Line!

Volunteer Benefits & Recruitment

The KidVenture control line program continually welcomes new and returning volunteers of all skill levels. Benefits include EAA AirVenture admission wristbands, parking passes, flexible 4-hour morning or afternoon shifts (leaving plenty of time to enjoy the airshow), Camp Scholler camping community camaraderie, the annual Wednesday evening volunteer BBQ, and the thrill of mentoring the next generation of aviators. Sign up at circlemasters.com or via EAA KidVenture volunteer registration.

5. Champions, Demonstrations & Pulse Jet Flights

Between training flights, master pilots and aviation leaders participate on the circles:

  • Noisy Pulse Jet Flights: High-speed pulse jet demonstrations draw huge crowds across Pioneer Airport with their unmistakable, deafening roar and glowing tailpipes.
  • Todd Lee: Master stunt pilot performing high-precision aerobatic demonstration flights and mentoring youth.
  • Gilbert & Véronique Béringer: Founders of Beringer Aero and international competitors from France demonstrating European precision flying.
  • Tom Siegler: National Control Line Combat champion demonstrating high-speed maneuvering and reflexes.
  • Burt Rutan & Sean Elliott: Aircraft designer Burt Rutan and EAA VP of Advocacy & Safety Sean Elliott have visited and supported the flight line.
  • Precision Aerobatics (CL Stunt): Demonstrators fly the full AMA pattern (loops, inverted flight, eights, cloverleafs) on 60-foot lines.
